# ADR-127: Measure captain interventions by cause, with evidence and honest effort limits

**Status: accepted (ratified 2026-11-04).**

## Context

Prepared at the Captain's request on 2026-10-31.

The solo-captain alpha succeeds when a developer can run and recover the crew without founder knowledge (charter T8/T12, V3). The [Universe announcement](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-universe-2026-evolving-every-layer-of-the-stack/) motivates measuring completed work and the cost of delivering it.

Existing foundations are not new scope:
- `backend/internal/repodash/README.md` documents throughput, cycle time, cost per merged change, health and `WaitOnHuman`.
- ADR-122 D4 requires factory metrics for self-improvement; ADR-117 defines review-effectiveness measurement.
- Audit entries record approvals, escalations, amendments and recovery actions, but a decision count, a page count, a waiting interval and active human effort are different quantities.

The missing product question is: which recurring situations require the captain to intervene, and did a change reduce those interventions without degrading outcomes? Historic audit coverage is incomplete; shell actions outside Fishhawk and physical human presence cannot be inferred.

## Options

- **A. Existing latency and economics only.** Smallest scope; cannot distinguish mandatory approval from avoidable recovery.
- **B. Evidence-linked intervention episodes and a read-only cause breakdown (recommended).** Extend the existing repository dashboard surface; preserve unknowns and explain denominators.
- **C. Track active human time or produce one productivity score.** Reject for this proposal: browser dwell and waiting time are poor effort proxies, and a single score encourages suppressing useful scrutiny.

## Recommendation

**Option B.**

**D1. Observation unit.** An intervention episode is one server-observed decision or recovery need for a repository-local run, stage/gate occurrence and episode generation in ADR-125 D7's sense ("A revived or reopened run starts a new episode generation."). An intervention episode is this record's counting unit and is distinct from an ADR-125 episode, which it uses only for generation boundaries. The initial projection is explicitly labelled run-scoped: run-less ratification, security disposition and other repository-level decisions are outside its population and are not represented as zero intervention. Repeated notifications, reads, retries of the same write and duplicate audit observations do not create new episodes. A new gate occurrence after a revision is a new episode, unless D2 folds it into the episode of the amendment or recovery event that opened it. The versioned mapping (D2) defines, for each supported need source, its canonical episode key, opening condition, action and resolution references, and occurrence and generation boundaries. Multiple observations of that key contribute one episode. A concern batch sharing one opening entry (`backend/internal/concern/postgres.go:44-50`) is one gate need, and so one episode; each concern's disposition is an action on it, and per-concern statistics stay with ADR-117. A batch episode resolves when every concern of the episode, across every reviewer's batch for that gate occurrence, has a recorded disposition, at the time of the last one; it also closes, with a distinct closure kind rather than a disposition, at the run's merge for concerns stamped `open_at_merge` (ADR-117 D1) and at the terminal transition of its generation when that generation fails or is cancelled, at that transition's recorded time. A later reopened concern keeps the episode's key and returns it to unresolved. The first slice derives the key from the existing audit entry's run, stage and sequence, using the sequence of the need's opening entry as the mapping names it; later observations of the same need attach to that key. Several reviewer-terminal entries for one gate occurrence are one need: the mapping names the first such entry as the opening entry for the episode's key, and the last such entry of the occurrence as the opening time D4's wait-to-first-response measures from, because the gate is not actionable before every configured reviewer has reported; an occurrence whose last reviewer never reports has no opening time, and its wait is censored, never measured from the first entry. The generation ordinal is derived at the opening entry's sequence from the run's own revive and reopen rows (`backend/internal/audit/categories.go:655-656`) preceding it, named in the versioned mapping. No generation is stored and no new identity is stored. Observations with no reliable key are excluded from episode totals and ratios and shown in a separate diagnostic count; actions without a reliable episode reference remain unmatched.

Separate:
- **need:** a gate/page/recovery condition the server observed;
- **action:** an authenticated response to it, with actor kind derived from the recorded subject by the server's single agent-subject classification (`backend/internal/server/actor.go:43-46`), not from the stored audit actor kind alone, and delegated status;
- **resolution:** the recorded disposition, or unresolved.

Do not count a page as a response, a delegated response as a human action, or a human credential as proof of physical attention. Preserve the credential-attribution limits of ADR-113/123. Historical actions that cannot be linked remain visible as unmatched actions, except that a security-tied one follows D5's caller rule.

**D2. Closed classifications, with evidence.** Report expected gated approvals, meaning approvals at a gate occurrence the run's pinned spec declares and that no amendment or recovery event opened, separately from exceptional intervention, and show the mapping version beside every total. A gate occurrence that an amendment or recovery event opened counts once, as that event's episode. Initial exceptional classes: scope amendment, reviewer/requirements arbitration, policy or budget exception, infrastructure recovery, verification failure recovery, and integration/conflict recovery; include `unknown`, the state of a class that cannot be determined. The primary class is derived from the originating event kind by a versioned mapping. Related events may be secondary tags, but do not multiply the primary total. Counting cases this record does not state are fixed by the versioned mapping and pinned by its replay fixtures; the mapping version is shown beside every total. An agent explanation reaches the view only through an existing crew-message or finding surface, rendered as labelled advice; the first slice adds no model call for it (D6). "Repeated scope amendments" is an observation, not proof of planner fault. Human corrections are not part of the first slice, and this record decides no record type for them. A later decision must name its append-only record, its authorized write path and its effective-version rule, and it cites source references without rewriting source facts. A new write path needs its own decision. Security attribution changes remain exclusively subject to ADR-126 D5.

**D3. Cohorts and denominators.**
- Display needs opened in the window, unresolved carry-in, resolved episodes and unmatched actions separately.
- "Interventions per merged change" is a repository-level ratio only, reported as two figures over the same eligible-change denominator: exceptional episodes per merged change, and expected gated approvals per merged change. They are never summed into one headline figure. An eligible merged change is a distinct PR first merged in the window that has at least one recorded run lineage (a `pr_merged` row on a run of this repository, `backend/internal/audit/categories.go:617`); a merged PR with no recorded run lineage is outside the run-scoped population and is never a zero-episode change. The first slice's collector enumerates merged changes from run audit rows only, so it does not observe such PRs and the view states that; a count of them needs a forge-side listing and is a direction. Every eligible merged change counts in the denominator, including one with zero observed episodes. For it, the server collector selects distinct PRs first merged in the window and retrieves their recorded run lineage independently of run creation time. It counts only episodes attributable to that delivered change through the merge. The versioned mapping defines the accepted retry, recovery and decomposition links and their traversal boundaries; a shared issue or ordinary follow-up link alone does not establish change attribution. Ambiguous attribution is excluded from the numerator and reported as incomplete lineage. Collapse decomposition and retry lineage so the same PR is one change. State the observation cutoff and exclusions; this is a descriptive ratio, not a failure probability.
- Unresolved carry-in is enumerated independently of the dashboard look-back. Any bounded or unavailable enumeration makes the affected population explicitly incomplete; existing dashboard scan completion is not proof of completeness for these cohorts, because that scan does not count a run created more than `LookBack` before the window and merged inside it (`backend/internal/repodash/README.md:18-19`).
- Report failed and cancelled attempts whose recorded terminal transition falls in the selected window; an attempt is one run generation (D1), so a revived run's failed generation stays a failed attempt, shown as revived, and its live generation is reported in the still-running inventory at the observation cutoff, regardless of creation time. Missing transition evidence makes temporal membership unknown and is reported separately, never silently excluded. A failed attempt later followed by a merged retry through the mapping's accepted links is shown as retried. Whether a change as a whole was abandoned is a direction. Post-merge recovery is a separate cohort linked to its original change.
- Segment by repository, workflow name and spec revision, stage, and, where present, ADR-122 D1's `origin: self_improvement/<class>` and the security-persona attachment of the latest review round of the episode's stage at its opening entry's sequence; any other change-class segmentation is a direction, not decided here. Segments apply to episode counts, each episode taking the segment of its own run and stage, and not to the merged-change ratio. A run whose source item carries no recorded origin marker reads `none`, a review with no persona attachment reads `not_attached`, and a stage with no review round at the opening entry's sequence reads `no_round`; `unknown` is reserved for a segment value the source cannot determine. The origin segment reads `unknown`, not `none`, whenever the source item cannot be read. The read from run to source item is part of the first slice's collector, through the repository's declared work-management provider; a read that fails or is unavailable yields `unknown`. Do not compare runs with different segment values as one population; these segments do not grade how hard a change is.
- Surface incomplete scans, retention gaps, unknown attribution, unlinked lineage and missing actor metadata beside every total. An empty, unusable or below-minimum cell is `insufficient_data` for medians and ratios, not zero effort; counts remain shown. The cell minimum is a documented product default.

**D4. Time and outcome safeguards.** For each episode, wait-to-first-response runs from the recorded opening to its first linked authenticated response, with actor kind and delegated status shown. Resolution time is separate. Still-open episodes without a linked authenticated response remain censored at the observation cutoff. Episodes that resolve, cancel or are superseded without such a response display their recorded closure time and `no_response_observed`, with no measured response duration. Closure is never substituted for a response, and missing response evidence never becomes zero. Dispatch and merge timestamps are not response substitutes, and queue and dispatch delay must not become response delay. The existing `WaitOnHuman` rollup (`backend/internal/repodash/README.md:37-38`), two of whose three gates close on dispatch or merge timestamps (`backend/internal/latency/latency.go:8-21`), is not inherited and remains a separately labelled approximation. Do not estimate active work minutes or money saved from timestamps. Initial delivery adds no time tracker and no staff ranking. Show acceptance coverage and failed and cancelled attempts for the stated intervention cohorts, with explicit denominators; the health fold's population of runs created in the window (`backend/internal/repodash/README.md:26`) is not inherited. ADR-117 escape/review signals retain their own eligibility, minimums and observation-lag rules. Label any population mismatch, and display unavailable or insufficient evidence explicitly; unmatched or immature signals cannot substantiate an outcome-preserving comparison. Direction, not decided here: a before/after comparison would state configuration versions, sample sizes, observation lags and confounds; correlation never certifies a process improvement.

**D5. Surface and authority.** Add a bounded "Interventions" view to the existing repository bridge/dashboard, grouped by closed class and segment, with drill-down to authorized source evidence and existing next actions; this record decides no recurrence threshold. Do not create a new command surface. Classification and per-caller recompute are server-side only, and the Web UI renders fields it is given. The route is registered under the authentication tier and repo-visibility gate of the existing rollup routes it extends (`backend/internal/server/repodash.go:23-26`) and is documented in the OpenAPI surface. No metric grants delegation, waives a concern or changes a gate.

Access:
- Before classification or aggregation the server applies account and repository isolation plus each source's read restrictions, including ADR-126 confidentiality. These checks govern counts, denominators, tags, links and caches. A cache is keyed to the caller's scope.
- The first slice refuses run-bound `mcp:run:*` subjects on this view.
- For a security-tied episode, or a security-tied action or observation that has no episode (one whose source row is a `category=security` concern, a crew finding from the `security` role, or a security finding, or whose opening or resolving reference resolves to one), a caller who passes ADR-126 D5's gate sees class and location references only; any other caller gets those items excluded from every episode count, every unmatched-action and diagnostic count, and every denominator that counts them, while the merged change itself stays in its cohort. An item whose security tie cannot be read is treated as security-tied and excluded for a caller without ADR-126 D5 access (fail closed). For every caller without ADR-126 D5 access, whether or not any such item exists, the view states that restricted sources are excluded and gives no number, so the statement's presence discloses nothing.
- The first slice writes nothing to the tracker or the site, and renders only on the authenticated bridge. Any later public export needs its own decision and an operator-reviewed, allow-listed projection, following the pattern of ADR-125 D6.

**D6. Portability and implementation.** The event mapping is product logic; population windows and display limits are repository declarations/defaults per ADR-110. The configuration surface is fixed in the first slice's spec reference. Population windows and display limits carry documented product defaults, so a missing declaration is a missing override (ADR-110 rule 4) and reads as the default; a population the defaults cannot produce reads `insufficient_data`; neither is a Fishhawk fallback. Start with deterministic folds over existing events, reporting a class they cannot determine as `unknown`. Add new capture only where the first read-only walk demonstrates a gap. No new event store or model call is required for the first slice; a cache, if added, is a rebuildable projection.

**Validation.** Exercise mandatory approvals, delegated approvals, repeated pages, multi-round amendments, cancellation, recovery children, decomposed PRs, unknown actors, incomplete scans and empty cohorts. A replay fixture with exact expected results covers two distinct needs on one stage; several observations of one need; one opening entry with several concerns, which is one episode with one action per disposition; a partially dispositioned batch, asserting unresolved status and then, at its last disposition, the closure time; and two reviewer-terminal entries for one gate occurrence, which are one need, unresolved until the last concern across both reviewers' batches has a disposition, and whose wait-to-first-response starts at the last reviewer's entry. A generation fixture revives a run and reopens a PR-closed run, and asserts that a need opened before the revive keeps its generation and a need opened after it starts a new one. A classification fixture has an approval at a declared gate both before and after a scope amendment on one run, and asserts the expected and exceptional counts, including that the gate occurrence the amendment opened counts once, as the amendment's episode, and adds no expected approval. An old-run fixture, a run created before the dashboard look-back and merged inside the window, stays in its merged-change cohort. A lineage fixture covers two related runs producing different PRs, alongside genuine retry and decomposition cases. A zero-episode fixture has an eligible merged change with no observed episode, and asserts that it counts in the ratio's denominator. Attempt fixtures cover a pre-PR failure, old live work and a failure followed by successful recovery. A response fixture covers one still-open episode with no response and one closed without a response. A non-Fishhawk fixture exercises the production declaration resolver, server collector and rendered projection. It verifies invariant classifications for equivalent evidence, changed population and display behavior under changed declarations, and the documented defaults or named failure behavior when declarations are absent. An event replay must not inflate counts. Queue and dispatch delay must not appear as response delay. Removing source access must remove detail without leaking it through aggregate totals: a cached projection is never served across caller scopes and is rebuilt when any source's read restriction or the caller's access changes, and the restricted-sources statement renders for a caller without ADR-126 D5 access on a window with zero security-tied items exactly as it does on one with many. A repository-readable caller who fails ADR-126 D5's gate finds security-tied items, and items whose security tie cannot be read, in no count or denominator, and a run-bound `mcp:run:*` subject is refused. A caller of another account, and a caller without forge read access to the repository, are refused with the family's point-read DENY, and a repository-visibility fault refuses rather than serving a partial projection.

## Decision

**Accepted 2026-11-04** by the Captain (1 of 1; manual ratification path, the ADR-113 D2 verb is not yet built; approver and hashes in the ratification comment). **Option B**, i.e. D1–D3, D5, D6 and D4's wait timing and outcome display as stated in the Recommendation, decided now. D4's before/after comparison, any new event capture and change-level abandonment are directions, not decided. Human attribution corrections are deferred. This record amends no accepted record and changes no runtime permission.

## Consequences

- **Value:** makes charter V3 actionable with a descriptive view that is outside ADR-122 D4 item 4's fixed factory-metric list, without claiming measured human effort.
- **Costs:** historical event normalization, the episode-key mapping, lineage joining, a bounded bridge view, and a repository-scoped enumeration of merged-PR rows and unresolved needs by time, which may need its own index. No current query supplies it: the audit category query is per run (`backend/internal/audit/queries.sql:29-32`), the cross-run feed filters by category, run and account but not repository or time (`backend/internal/audit/queries.sql:104-109`), the decision index omits `pr_merged` (`backend/internal/decisionindex/decisionindex.go:46-56`), and the digest's repository-scoped reads cover its three page categories and parked stages on non-terminal runs (`backend/internal/digest/store.go:102`, `backend/internal/digest/store.go:134`, `backend/internal/digest/store.go:230`).
- **Residuals:** off-platform effort is invisible; credential identity is not physical presence; required approvals may be valuable; the initial projection is labelled run-scoped, so run-less ratification, security disposition and other repository-level decisions are outside its population, not zero intervention; fewer interventions can mean worse oversight or suppressed requests (an agent that works around scope records no amendment), so this record offers an intervention count only as a descriptive observation, never as ADR-125 D1 evidence, and does not make it a judge input; whether an ADR-122 D4 item 6 registration may cite it, paired with an outcome measure, is decided by that registration's approver under ADR-121, not by this record.
- **Sequence:** On ratification, these are decided: D1–D3, D5 and D6, and D4's wait timing and outcome display. D4's before/after comparison, any new event capture and change-level abandonment (D3) are directions until the first read-only walk has run. Read-only breakdown and an external-repository walk first; capture gaps second; comparisons only after enough comparable history exists. This is a scoped alpha candidate under T8/T12 and V3, not a new alpha exit criterion.
- **Deferred:** human attribution corrections, whose later decision must name an append-only record, an authorized write path and an effective-version rule.
- **Relationships:** related to ADR-122 D4 item 4 (factory metrics) but not an amendment. It adds no precondition and changes no enablement condition, evaluator registration or delegation. It supersedes none. ADR-117 remains the authority for review-effectiveness statistics.
