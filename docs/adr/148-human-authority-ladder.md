# ADR-148: The human-authority ladder: three credential tiers and the first presence adoptions

**Status: accepted (ratified 2026-11-14).**

## Context

Prepared at the Captain's request on 2026-11-14, from the ratified-ADR coherence review of that date. Repository citations are to `main` at `4e40a1e96`.

The accepted records agree that some decisions belong to a human. They disagree about what shows that a human took one.

**Three credential rules.**
- ADR ratification admits only an `oauth` API token: "Static tokens, `fho_` authorization-server tokens and cookie sessions are refused" (`docs/adr/113-forge-verified-adr-ratification.md:69-70`). An SSH signature under the forge-published keys is the second method (`:73`).
- Stage approval gates that declare `approvals.members` admit `session`, `oauth` and `oauth_as` (`docs/adr/123-forge-verified-stage-approvals.md:53`). ADR-126 D5 and production approval use the same three (`docs/adr/126-security-finding-record.md:102`; `docs/adr/132-release-candidates-and-promotion.md:77`).
- Clearing a server-check concern admits `session` only (`docs/adr/143-human-only-actions-credential-kind.md:37`), because "The server cannot tell a CLI call from a stdio MCP call: both present the same stored bearer credential" (`:61`).

By ADR-143's own reasoning an `oauth` bearer is agent-drivable. ADR-084 Amendment A1 found the case on this repository: "An MCP server configured with a human-bound credential and driven by an agent session presents a human subject, so the guard admits it." (`docs/adr/084-reviewer-personas.md:76`). ADR-143 D4 keeps that setup supported (`docs/adr/143-human-only-actions-credential-kind.md:67`).

**The captain seat.** ADR-083 rule 4 lets anyone satisfying the strictest human gate predicate claim an empty seat, recorded as unverified where that predicate is weak (`docs/adr/083-captain-semantics.md:56`). Rule 7 lets the incoming captain confirm or lower delegation, and "Nothing raises" (`:59`). ADR-143 excluded the seat, delegation and first-officer seat verbs from its test until ADR-083 is amended (`docs/adr/143-human-only-actions-credential-kind.md:55`).

**Proof of presence.** ADR-144 decides a WebAuthn step-up and adopts it for no action: its registry is "empty here", and "An action is added by an amendment of the record that governs it" (`docs/adr/144-proof-of-presence.md:39`). ADR-145 decides recovery and still "builds nothing by itself" (`docs/adr/145-proof-of-presence-recovery.md:49`).

**Surfaces.** ADR-019 adopted "every action it surfaces must also be reachable from CLI, GitHub, and Claude Code" (`docs/adr/019-fishhawk-as-coordination-layer-not-destination.md:40`), and ADR-143 decides "The Web UI cookie session is the human channel" for its actions (`docs/adr/143-human-only-actions-credential-kind.md:66`).

**First officer.** ADR-097 D3 places the trigger set "in the workflow spec's `operator_agent` block" (`docs/adr/097-operator-as-orchestrator.md:94`), on which ADR-089 D1 also conditions a delegated attach (`docs/adr/089-first-officer-read-only-crew-seat.md:53`), and workflow-v2 removed that block (`docs/spec/workflow-v2.md:1858`).

## Options

- **A. Decide each action in its own record, as ADR-143's Directions list them.** Five or more amendments, each choosing a rule with no common scale. The inversion stays until the last one lands.
- **B. One strongest rule for every human-only action.** Session plus presence everywhere. A fresh local install could not approve a plan for a day, and a static-subject install never could.
- **C. One ladder of three tiers, each action assigned by consequence, presence adopted in a stated order (recommended).**

## Recommendation

**Option C.**

**D1. The ladder.** Every action this record places sits on one of three tiers. A tier adds a requirement to the action's existing guards and removes none, except ADR-113 D2(a)'s refusal of a cookie session, which D4 replaces: scope, account, eligibility, quorum, and the agent-subject and delegated-request refusals stay in front (ADR-143 D1, ADR-144 D1). A higher tier is adopted by an amendment of the action's governing record. No repository or deployment declaration places an action on a tier until ADR-144 Direction 7 is decided; this record introduces none. An action this record does not place keeps the guard its governing record states, and is not thereby on a tier. An action named in a Direction is undecided: its verb does not ship, or keeps its present guard where it already exists, until an amendment places it. Charter lease issue is placed no lower than tier 1 by that amendment, because issuing attaches a seat (D8); the tier of recovering a suspended lease is Direction (a).

**D2. Tier 0: routine gates.** Plan approval and ordinary stage approval keep the credential rule ADR-123 decides, except a plan approval that is an ADR-121 tier-I adoption and a production-class approval (D4): a forge-verified kind on a gate that declares `approvals.members`, and the gate's subject test alone on a gate that declares none. This record changes neither. Accepted alpha residual, stated here: a tier-0 action is agent-drivable. A process holding a human-bound credential is indistinguishable from the human, and that includes an operator agent approving a plan over MCP under the Captain's identity. Tier 0 therefore shows at most that an eligible identity's credential was used, and on a gate with no members leg only that the subject test passed, not that a person acted.

**D3. Tier 1: human judgements.** A tier-1 action is admitted only on a `session` credential, by ADR-143 D1's test. This record adds these to ADR-143 D2's closed list:
- the dispositions ADR-126 D5 lists under 'What needs the gate', the D2 `closed_unpublished` disposition and the D6 `fixed` attestation, narrowing their predicate from three kinds to `session`. Reading the confidential intake text (ADR-126 D3) keeps the three-kind predicate;
- claiming an empty captain seat (ADR-083 rule 4), subject to D6;
- attaching a first-officer seat (D8).

Clearing a server-check concern is already on the list. A tier-1 claim of the seat is shown as unproven on every surface that shows the captain, separately from ADR-083's unverified claim and `identity_verified` marks, until the holder completes, after the claim, a tier-2 delegation confirmation of any workflow on that repository that carries a proof; a confirmation taken on D6's path does not clear it.

**D4. Tier 2: authority-changing acts.** A tier-2 action needs a `session` credential and a proof of presence (ADR-144, ADR-145). The actions:
- offering and accepting a captain handover (ADR-083 rule 3);
- confirming delegation (ADR-083 rule 7);
- adopting an ADR-121 tier-I change (ADR-121 D5), with the proof carried on the plan approval that is the adoption, or on the adoption gate of a plan-less workflow. Every raise of delegation is a human-authored workflow-spec change (ADR-083 rule 7, ADR-085 rule 1); there is no separate raise verb. Where that change is made through a run it is an ADR-121 tier-I change and is adopted this way, with a new proof for each renewed adoption (ADR-121 D5). The protected-scope authorization of a plan-less workflow keeps its present rule. A change merged on the forge outside a run is covered only by ADR-121 D4.2, a stated residual. The merge-side forge approval (ADR-121 D4.2) is read from the forge and is unchanged;
- ratifying an ADR (ADR-113 D2), with verdict `accept` or `reject`. Method (a) becomes a Web UI session of the approving subject plus a proof whose challenge commits to the canonical statement ADR-113 D2 defines, not a separate one, whose subject equals the completing session's, and whose `expires` is at most ADR-144 D2's bound. `ratify.methods` gains `presence`; once this entry is enabled a ratification completes only by `presence`, whatever the repository declares. Only an `oauth` bearer of a non-agent subject, or a Web UI session, that passes ADR-113 D2's route authorization opens or joins the pending ratification; a bearer never completes it. The SSH signature of method (b) stays as an optional second artefact over the same statement and is never sufficient alone. ADR-113 D2's pending ratification is the pending decision of ADR-144 D4 for this action and keeps `ratify.pending_ttl`; discarding it is a stop under D5. The ADR-113 D2 verb's enabling change carries the SSH artefact's route, the statement's account binding and the methods migration, within these rules. This entry waits for that verb; the manual path (ADR-113 D5, D6) is unchanged by this record and carries no proof;
- approving a production-class deployment intent (ADR-132 D4), each counted approval carrying its own proof whose challenge commits to the approval statement ADR-132 D4 defines. A recovery that requires a new intent (ADR-132 D7) is such an approval and is tier 2; a previously approved automatic rollout recovery within an intent needs no new approval. Rejecting an intent and cancelling its run stay under D5. Where no approver can give a proof, the remaining route is ADR-132 D5's break-glass, recorded as external recovery;
- retiring a campaign-scoped or run-scoped first-officer seat early, and changing a seat's budget (D8).

This record is the adopting amendment ADR-144 D3 requires for each of them. This record accepts every authenticator ADR-144 D1 accepts; narrowing is ADR-144 Direction 8. For all of them:
- an identity that does not meet ADR-145 R1 is refused, never waived. A forge-session identity enrols first; a static subject cannot enrol (ADR-144 D6) and is governed by D6;
- removing or disabling an entry raises no server-synthesized concern; an entry is removed only by an amendment of this record (ADR-144 D3);
- a pending decision (ADR-144 D4) may be opened by a bearer that holds the action's existing write scope, or by a Web UI session that could complete the action but for the proof; a run-bound token cannot open one, and the charter identity cannot open one for an action ADR-101 D3 lists;
- a bearer opener, keyed by credential subject, holds at most five open pending decisions per repository, where open means not completed, lapsed, voided, discarded or withdrawn; a session opener has its own allowance of five per identity per repository, and exhausting the bearer allowance never blocks it. A pending decision lapses 24 hours after it is opened, 72 hours for an ADR-121 tier-I adoption; a ratification keeps `ratify.pending_ttl` (ADR-113 D2) and a production approval the intent's own lifetime, the approval expiry its statement binds (`docs/adr/132-release-candidates-and-promotion.md:75`). A pending decision may be discarded without a proof by any Web UI session of an identity eligible to complete it. A bearer may withdraw a pending decision only when it was opened on a bearer credential and the withdrawing subject equals the recorded opener subject; a bearer cannot withdraw a session-opened decision, even when the subjects match.

**Adoption order.** Each entry, tier 1 or tier 2, takes effect with its own enabling change. That change carries the Web UI session path ADR-143's Enablement requires and, for a tier-2 entry, the enrolment-readiness inventory, migration path, safe-to-ship statement and statement of what stays held that ADR-144's Enablement prerequisites require (`docs/adr/144-proof-of-presence.md:56`), with an estimate of how often the proof is asked on this repository. Tier-2 entries are enabled in this order: (1) handover and delegation confirmation, with the tier-1 seat claim enabled in the same change or before it; (2) ADR-121 tier-I adoption; (3) ratification; (4) production approval, when a production environment exists. Until an entry's enabling change lands, the action keeps the rule its governing record states today. The first-officer acts follow step 1.

**D5. Stops are never gated above any human tier.** Lowering delegation is the recorded lowering act of ADR-083 rule 7 and is admitted on any human credential the action accepts today. The workflow-spec change that makes it effective is an ADR-121 tier-I change. It needs no proof, and keeps every other rule of ADR-121 D5 (a non-agent approval, never a delegated or standing-order one), when a fail-closed check shows that its only effect is to reduce delegation. ADR-084 rule 5's drift check is necessary to that and not sufficient: the fail-closed check also compares the resolved autonomy settings of every affected workflow and gate, inherited paging obligations included, and finds no edit to any other field or tier-I path; a change it cannot classify needs D4's proof. A plan approval meets the check only where the plan carries a machine-checkable, base-bound statement of the exact reductions and the check verifies those reductions before approval. That adoption is conditional on the complete resulting diff matching the statement at the first post-implement gate, at merge, and at every re-check point ADR-121 D3 lists. A plan-less adoption applies the check to its exact head. A head that fails the check is held as ADR-121 D4 holds a tier-I change with no valid adoption, until an adoption that carries D4's proof. Step 2 is not enabled until its enabling change carries this check. An adoption taken on this path is scoped outside the entry's requirement (ADR-144 D3), recorded with the classification the check returned, and shown as unproven on every surface that shows it. Lowering delegation (as above), revoking or suspending a charter lease, retiring a lease-scoped first-officer seat, cancelling a run or campaign, withdrawing a handover offer, and rejecting a plan or a deployment intent, and discarding or withdrawing a pending decision as D4 states, are admitted on any human credential the action accepts today; engaging the host-local kill switch needs no credential; releasing it resumes charter dispatch, which ADR-101 gives the captain, and its tier is Direction (a). No tier above 0 is ever placed on the stopping acts listed above; releasing the kill switch and resuming a lease are outside this ceiling, and their tiers remain Direction (a).

**D6. Identity.**
- A static subject remains accepted for the seat (ADR-083 rule 5) until the end condition below. Until then, on a deployment where forge sign-in is not configured for the repository's forge (`backend/internal/server/auth.go:38-51` answers `oauth_unconfigured`), every tier-1 and tier-2 entry this record adds keeps the rule its governing record stated before this record, the empty-seat claim included. Clearing a server-check concern is not among them and stays as ADR-143 decides. Each entry is scoped so that this path is outside its requirement (ADR-144 D3), and every decision taken that way is recorded and shown as unproven on every surface that shows it; a static captain is also shown with `identity_verified: false` (ADR-083 rule 5). No other repository or deployment is exempted by it. Once forge sign-in has been configured for a repository's forge while an entry is enabled, that forge's exemption does not return, whether or not anyone has signed in. A later start without that configuration holds the enabled tier-1 and tier-2 entries for repositories on that forge, records the change durably, and shows it where the captain is shown; D5's actions stay available. Configuring forge sign-in applies every enabled entry at once, and each tier-2 action is held until an identity meets ADR-145 R1.
- On a deployment with forge sign-in, a static incumbent may offer the seat to a named forge-session identity under the offer's present guard; the acceptance is tier 2, so the successor enrols first. Where the deployment has not declared what ADR-144 D9 and ADR-145 R6 require, the acceptance is held and the incumbent keeps the seat; the first-step enabling change names in its safe-to-ship statement every act that stays held with it. The handover is recorded and shown as unproven on the handover itself, because its offer carried no proof; the successor, whose acceptance carried one, is not shown as an unproven captain. The end condition is reached for a deployment when it runs a product release at or after the first one that ships forge sign-in by default, which the change shipping that default records. From then the first bullet's exemption ends, whether or not the deployment has configured sign-in. From then a static subject can no longer claim the seat; a static incumbent keeps it until it hands over under this bullet.
- No agent identity offers, accepts, claims or holds the seat (ADR-083 rule 4, unchanged).

**D7. Surfaces (amends ADR-019).** This record removes a surface from no action except tier-1 and tier-2 actions; ADR-019's reachability rule and the exceptions other records state are otherwise unchanged. A tier-2 action may be opened from another surface as a pending decision and completes only in the Web UI. A tier-1 action has no hand-off and is taken in the Web UI. The hand-off exists only for actions this ladder expressly adopts. The enforceable boundary is a session that passes the server's CSRF protection (ADR-143 D1's four properties, `docs/adr/143-human-only-actions-credential-kind.md:41`); it is not a claim about which program sent the request.

**D8. First officer (amends ADR-089 D1, ADR-097 D3 and D5).**
- The trigger set is a fixed product default, all enabled. It is not declared in a workflow spec and is not changed per repository or mid-campaign. This replaces ADR-097 D3's `operator_agent` location and D5's first bullet.
- Attaching a seat is a tier-1 captain act. No agent attaches a seat. This replaces ADR-089 D1's delegated-attach clause.
- Retiring a campaign-scoped or run-scoped seat before its campaign or run ends is tier 2: it removes oversight and is not a stop in D5's sense. Retiring a lease-scoped seat halts charter actions (ADR-101 D8), so it is a stop under D5. Changing a seat's budget after attach, in either direction, is tier 2 when a verb for it exists; none exists today (`backend/internal/server/first_officer_seat.go`; its routes are attach, read and retire, `backend/internal/server/handlers.go:178-180`).
- Issuing a charter lease attaches its lease-scoped seat automatically, as ADR-101 D8 already decides (`docs/adr/101-charter-authorization-mode.md:135`); no separate attach act is needed.

**Directions (not decided here).**
- **(a) Charter lease issue, recovery and release.** The tier of issuing a lease, of recovering a suspended one, and of releasing the host-local kill switch. ADR-143 requires this before the lease verbs ship (`docs/adr/143-human-only-actions-credential-kind.md:78`); revocation is covered by D5. Whether lease issue or resumption also needs a confinement attestation belongs to ADR-149 (#5275), not to this record.
- **(c) Relinquishing the seat.** Its tier; it lowers the actor's own authority and also empties the seat. Until it is placed, relinquish keeps its present guard, so a relinquish followed by a tier-1 claim moves the seat without a proof: the tier-2 handover entries protect the seat only as far as the tier-1 claim does, and the page to the previous captain (ADR-083 rule 4) and the unproven mark are the controls.
- **(d) Other human acts.** ADR-143 exclusion (iv) dispositions, ADR-117 labelling and the escape-closure attestation, and ADR-139's rulings, waivers and errata.
- **(e) Seat token delivery.** The supported transfer of the token minted by a session attach to the seat's process must be decided and validated before session-only attachment is enabled. Enablement demonstrates an attached, authenticated seat performing its standing watch, not only a minted token or attachment audit row.
- **(f) Tier 0 hardening.** Whether plan approval moves to tier 1 after alpha.

## Decision

**Accepted 2026-11-14** by the Captain (1 of 1; manual ratification path, the ADR-113 D2 verb is not yet built; approver, erratum and hashes in the ratification comment). **Option C**, i.e. D1 to D8 as stated in the Recommendation, decided now: every human action this record places sits on one of three tiers (0: any forge-verified human credential; 1: a Web UI session; 2: a session plus proof of presence), presence is adopted in the stated order, stops are never gated above any human tier, and static identity keeps today's rules until local installs ship forge sign-in by default. This record is the adopting amendment ADR-144 D3 requires for each tier-2 entry, effective for an entry only with its enabling change. It amends ADR-019, ADR-083, ADR-089, ADR-097, ADR-101, ADR-113, ADR-121, ADR-123, ADR-126, ADR-132 and ADR-143 only as stated. The Directions are not decided. This record builds nothing by itself.

## Consequences

- **Scope.** This record builds nothing by itself. It is the adopting amendment ADR-144 D3 requires, and it takes effect for an entry only when that entry's enabling change carries what ADR-144's Enablement prerequisites name, on an ADR-144 and ADR-145 implementation that passes both records' Validation. Tier-1 entries need ADR-143's registry. Step 2 also waits for ADR-121 D5's adoption record, which is not built, and step 3 for the ADR-113 D2 verb.
- **What this amends.**
  - ADR-019: D7.
  - ADR-083: rules 3, 4 and 7 gain tiers (D3, D4); rule 5 gains an end condition and a migration (D6).
  - ADR-089 and ADR-097: D8.
  - ADR-101: lease issue takes no tier below 1 when it is placed (D1); revoking or suspending a lease and retiring its seat take none above 0 (D5); D3's non-delegable item "first-officer triggers or oversight budget (ADR-097 D5)" keeps only its budget half once the trigger set is fixed (D8).
  - ADR-113: D2's methods, as D4 states. ADR-139's statement that ratification keeps ADR-113 D2(a) and (b), and that a security disposition keeps ADR-126 D5's gate, is read as amended by D4 and D3.
  - ADR-121: D5's adoption carries a proof.
  - ADR-123: a plan approval that is an ADR-121 tier-I adoption, and a production-class approval, are narrowed from three kinds to `session` plus a proof.
  - ADR-126: D5's dispositions, the D2 `closed_unpublished` disposition and the D6 `fixed` attestation are narrowed to `session` (D3); reading the intake text keeps its predicate.
  - ADR-132: D4's counted approvals are narrowed from the three forge-verified kinds to `session` and each carries a proof.
  - ADR-143: D2's list grows by D3's entries. Exclusion (i) is resolved by D4. Exclusion (ii) is resolved for ADR-121 tier-I adoption and production-intent approval only. Exclusion (v) is resolved for ADR-126 only. Exclusion (vii) is resolved except for relinquish (Direction (c)); withdraw and delegation lower keep their present guards (D5). For those entries, D1's refusal and its Validation apply where D6 does not scope the request out; the server-check entry is not scoped by D6 and stays unconditional.
- **Costs.**
  - Tier-2 actions need a declared origin that ADR-144 D9 supports, two authenticators, a first-enrolment delay of at least 24 hours and a declared notification channel that meets ADR-145 R6. The default local stack's `127.0.0.1:8080` origin cannot run the ceremony until a supported origin, such as a loopback `localhost` origin, is declared (`docs/adr/144-proof-of-presence.md:45`).
  - A successor must enrol before a handover can complete.
  - Seat attach by a bearer call stops working once D8 is enforced.
  - A production rollback or roll-forward that needs a new intent is held while its approvers' presence is held (ADR-145 R3, R5, R6, R8); break-glass outside the product is then the only route.
  - Except for paths D5 and D6 scope out, step 2 asks for a proof at every ADR-121 tier-I adoption and again at every new head of a plan-less tier-I change (ADR-121 D5). A proof asked often is given without reading (ADR-144 Context); the step-2 enabling change reports the expected rate and does not ship above a rate the Captain accepts.
- **Residuals.**
  - Tier 0 is agent-drivable (D2).
  - Tier 1 stops an agent that holds only a bearer credential. It does not stop a process that can read the browser's cookie store or drive the browser (ADR-143's residuals, ADR-144 `docs/adr/144-proof-of-presence.md:44`); on a single-operator host it rests on how the browser profile is protected.
  - Tier 2 proves the enrolled authenticator's reported user verification for a bound decision, not that a person read or freshly approved it. First enrolment is trusted on first use; a software authenticator installed by the session holder is not detected, two enrolled credential IDs do not establish independent devices, and a browser-driving agent may complete verification using a known typed secret or a provider that requires no fresh gesture. These ADR-144 and ADR-145 residuals remain accepted. On the default single-host local stack, tier 2 adds no in-band protection against a process with a shell and a browser on the host; ADR-145's delay, notification and cancellation, which reach the person only on an off-host channel (R6), are the controls.
  - On the default single-host local stack, "A process with a shell on the deployment host ... holds the deployment" (`docs/adr/145-proof-of-presence-recovery.md:55`). The deployment-holder recovery of ADR-145 R4 is therefore reachable by an unconfined local agent. This is accepted for alpha; ADR-145's delay, notification and cancellation are the controls, and R6 requires a declared channel that is not loopback, an IP literal or the Web UI origin; it does not show the endpoint is off the host.
  - Presence has no anchor outside the deployment (`docs/adr/144-proof-of-presence.md:41`). The optional SSH signature gives an anchor outside the deployment only for a ratification that carries it; a ratification without one rests on the deployment's audit chain.
  - Until relinquish is placed (Direction (c)), a relinquish followed by a tier-1 claim moves the seat without a proof, and the page to the previous captain (ADR-083 rule 4) and the unproven mark are the controls.
  - A raise of delegation merged on the forge outside a run carries no proof (ADR-121 D4.2). On a plan-less workflow the protected-scope authorization carries none; only the adoption does. After step 3 a repository that does not declare `types.adr.ratify` still ratifies by the manual path, with no proof, until an amendment of ADR-113 ends that path.
  - Until each enabling change lands, today's rules apply. Ratification today is the manual path, which carries no credential rule; ADR-113 D2 keeps its two methods until its verb and step 3 land.
  - The record that ends D6's exemption is deployment state; a deployment holder who can rewrite it restores the exemption, and the audit chain is the only trace.
  - A lowering that D5's check cannot classify is held for a proof. Where presence is unavailable, its routes are a change the check can classify, a merge on the forge outside a run (ADR-121 D4.2), and D5's other stops, which need none.

### Validation

- **D1:** the ADR-143 D2 list and the ADR-144 D3 registry, or one mark in either, together list every tiered action with its tier and each Direction-listed action with its interim guard, with one source of truth per action. A test fails when a handler that refuses agent subjects is in neither a registry entry nor a recorded ADR-143 exclusion, and another when an entry is enforced before its enabling change.
- **D2:** a tier-0 approval over MCP on a human-bound credential is admitted and recorded with its credential kind.
- **D3:** Where the entry is enforced and D6 does not scope the request out, each tier-1 action is refused on `oauth`, `oauth_as`, `static`, `mcp_run`, `unrecorded` and `unknown` credentials through REST, the CLI and both MCP transports, and admitted on a session that passes CSRF; a tier-1 claim is shown unproven until the holder's first tier-2 confirmation after it.
- **D4:** Where the entry is enforced and neither D5 nor D6 scopes the request out, each tier-2 action is refused on a session without a valid proof, with a replayed proof, and for an identity that does not meet ADR-145 R1. A ratification with a valid SSH signature and no proof is refused; an ADR-121 tier-I plan approval without a proof is refused, and so is a renewed adoption on a new head without a new proof; a ratification with `reject` needs the proof, and one with a proof and an SSH signature records both; each counted production approval needs its own proof, and a recovery that needs a new intent is refused without one.
- **D4:** a pending decision opened by a run-bound token is refused; a sixth open decision by one opener is refused; a captain, delegation or first-officer decision older than 24 hours, or an ADR-121 tier-I adoption older than 72 hours, cannot complete; a ratification lapses at `ratify.pending_ttl` and a production approval at its intent's approval expiry; a session-started decision is admitted while the bearer opener holds five; the bearer and session allowances are each tested independently; a discard needs no proof.
