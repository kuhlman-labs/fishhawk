# ADR-132: Immutable release candidates and environment promotion

**Status: accepted (ratified 2026-11-04).**

## Context

Prepared at the Captain's request on 2026-10-31. The existing decisions cover delegated deploys (ADR-038), independent acceptance (ADR-049), release evidence and publication (ADR-051), and release readiness (ADR-079). They do not jointly guarantee that the artifact qualified in stage is the one approved and then observed in production.

The present release workflow dispatches `deploy.yml` at `main`. The deploy reconciler can observe the pipeline's `head_sha`, while a pipeline may actually deploy another image. The acceptance identity probe also permits an unverifiable target to proceed with a warning. Those facts cannot support a claim that a production artifact was qualified and approved merely because the pipeline is green.

A release also spans many bounded runs. It needs attributable links among the ratified brief, merged work, built artifacts, qualification, approval, actual deployment, and later observations without creating a second tracker or a project-wide execution state machine.

## Options

- **A. Keep ref-based deployment and reconstruct the release afterward.** Minimal work; mutable refs, rebuilds, and configuration drift make evidence and approval ambiguous.
- **B. Immutable candidate and deployment-intent contracts over existing runs and pipelines (recommended).** Fishhawk governs identity, evidence, and decisions; external systems retain build, rollout, credentials, and monitoring.
- **C. Own the build and deployment platform.** Rejected: crosses ADR-038 and MVP_SPEC §9's boundary and duplicates customer infrastructure.

## Recommendation

Adopt option B. The following are product contracts, not an assertion that the named records or wire fields exist today. Schema, persistence, endpoints, and callback authentication must be specified in implementation plans before they are exposed.

### D1. A candidate is an immutable manifest

A candidate identifies its repository, included change references, source commit(s), and exact component artifacts by content digest. The ratified release-brief revision and the qualification evidence are bound by the qualification attempt and the approval, not by the manifest hash: a brief revision change leaves the candidate hash unchanged and requires reassessment, and evidence accrued after the manifest is published is never manifest content. Include build provenance and signature-verification references, deployment package/chart digest, migration-set digest, compatibility assumptions, and declared feature/configuration expectations. A source SHA, tag, semver string, or successful pipeline alone is not an artifact identity.

The manifest has a canonical encoding and content hash. Any material field change creates a new revision/hash; published evidence is never edited into agreement with a newer candidate. A rebuild is a different candidate even when the source commit is unchanged. Human-readable version labels may point to candidates but never replace their hashes in authorization.

Initial scope is components within one repository. External dependencies are referenced by pinned identities and compatibility evidence; this introduces no cross-repository campaign or approval authority.

### D2. Environment registration is an authority boundary

An operator-controlled, versioned registration binds a stable target identity to the owning repository, environment class (`development`, `qualification`, or `production`), allowed delegate/pipeline identity, target identity verifier, configuration-reference policy, and external credential binding names. Customer display names are independent of class. Unknown targets/classes refuse guarded deployment; `prod`, `production`, and hostname aliases cannot lower the class. Disposable preview targets used by pre-merge acceptance (ADR-049) are not guarded deployments and need no registration.

Registration is a repository declaration read from the default-branch blob and recorded by blob SHA. Its home is either the workflow spec file (a derived ADR-121 D2 binding) or a file under `.fishhawk/` whose path is declared in the workflow spec's `protected_paths` block (an ADR-121 D2 declared binding). Either way a registration change is a tier-I change adopted under ADR-121 D5 by a forge-verified non-agent identity (ADR-123). Until ADR-121's bindings, `protected_paths` declaration, D4 seams and D5 adoption gate are implemented for the repository, a registration, check suite or reuse rule has no tier-I enforcement point, and guarded production promotion is unavailable. The backend snapshots the declaration's blob SHA into each deployment intent. A candidate cannot register or reclassify the environment judging it. Secret values stay external; include only access-controlled opaque version references where behavior depends on them. Never store a digest of a low-entropy secret as a substitute for its value.

The effective registration and policy are snapshotted into each deployment intent and checked for current validity again at dispatch. Revocation holds undispatched work. In-flight revocation does not pretend to undo a side effect; it holds subsequent promotion, records the uncertainty, and routes recovery to the operator.

### D3. Qualification establishes release evidence, not deployment authority

A qualification attempt binds the candidate hash, ratified release-brief revision, release-criteria revision, environment identity and generation, effective non-secret configuration digest, check-suite revision, and evidence cutoff. It records each required criterion as passed, failed, or unknown, with bounded source references and limitations. Admitted producers are: the ADR-049/053 acceptance stage against a `qualification`-class target (the agent boundary of ADR-053 Decision §3 is unchanged: never a `production`-class target, and for an enabled target the staging-only binding reads the registered class, not the literal environment name); deterministic checks run by the registered delegate and reported through the D6 authenticated channel; and per-change CI or pre-merge preview acceptance evidence under the next paragraph's limits. No other producer counts, and no new executor is introduced. Before enabling guarded promotion for a release brief, validate that every pre-deployment blocking criterion has an admitted evidence producer. A criterion requiring human observations or other external evidence without an adopted ingestion and adjudication contract makes that brief unsupported for guarded promotion, with the criterion and missing capability named explicitly. Such evidence remains available to ADR-079 planning and readiness; an agent or CI summary cannot substitute for the original observation. Zero executed required checks, missing results, unavailable identity, or stale evidence cannot become a pass.

Per-change CI and pre-merge preview acceptance evidence (from an unregistered disposable target) may support a release criterion only through the explicit criterion mapping, and does not establish integration of the assembled candidate. Release qualification covers the release-blocking criteria the ratified brief declares (ADR-079 §1, required release evidence); this record enumerates no category list of its own. Scope is declared per release; this is not a fixed universal suite.

Fishhawk validates each submission's authenticated producer, candidate and target binding, criterion identity, governing revisions, completeness and freshness before recording qualification. Existing acceptance and CI evidence enters through an explicit mapping to release-brief criterion IDs, naming for each mapped criterion the producing stage and whether its criteria are the plan-time criteria of ADR-049 Recommendation 4 or the brief's own; an acceptance run that takes its criteria from the ratified brief rather than an approved plan is the ADR-049 change declared in Amendment scope. Fishhawk computes promotion eligibility from validated results under the pinned policy, and an agent-authored aggregate pass cannot substitute for missing evidence. The check suite, release criteria and evidence-reuse rules are Tier I under ADR-121: the criteria through the ratified brief (ADR-079 §3), the suite and reuse rules as repository declarations bound under ADR-121 D2 (in the workflow spec file, or declared in `protected_paths`), adopted before use; a candidate cannot change the suite that qualifies it.

Each attempt runs against a target registered with class `qualification`, with a controlled target generation. A concurrent replacement or relevant configuration change invalidates its observations. Evidence from a `development` or `production`-class target never satisfies a pre-deployment criterion. Development-layer evidence may be recorded as informational, but this does not change a criterion's ratified blocking status. A pre-deployment blocking criterion without an admissible qualification-class evidence mapping remains unsatisfied and holds guarded promotion until supported evidence satisfies it or a revised brief is human-ratified. A production-phase criterion of the same release gates only that release's verified completion or its next promotion step, under the adopted production verification policy. Different candidate, brief or criterion revisions require a new assessment. Evidence reuse is explicit and conservative: unchanged content, test inputs, environment contract, applicable dependencies, and freshness must be demonstrated by the registered criterion's reuse rule; absent such a rule, rerun. This is separate from ADR-096/124's source-tree verify-verdict transfer.

Initially, production dispatch requires every criterion designated as blocking pre-deployment promotion to pass. The ratified brief assigns each required criterion to its decision phase; production smoke and later observations remain pending until that phase and gate verified completion or subsequent promotion, without being counted as pre-deployment passes. Nonblocking findings and unknowns remain visible. Revising which criteria block is a new human-ratified brief/policy revision and triggers reassessment; it never relabels a historical failure as success. Identity, authorization, and evidence-authenticity controls are not waivable criteria. A production hotfix is not an exception to this contract: it is a candidate qualified and approved against a (possibly narrow) ratified brief revision (ADR-079 §3) under D3 and D4; urgency never reduces the blocking set, and the only alternative is D5's break-glass, recorded as external recovery.

### D4. Readiness, approval, execution, and verification are distinct claims

ADR-079 readiness consumes the exact brief, candidate, and qualification revisions and recommends ship, narrow, or wait. The recommendation grants no release authority. ADR-051 publication and version selection remain separate actions.

The production approval statement binds at least:

- candidate manifest hash, ratified release-brief revision, and readiness/qualification references;
- target registration revision and environment identity;
- delegate identity and immutable executable pipeline/configuration revision;
- effective target configuration, migration plan, and approved secret-version references where required;
- expected currently deployed predecessor and target generation;
- rollback/roll-forward procedure revision and the permitted recovery target;
- approval expiry and the adopted production verification policy revision.

A forge-verified, non-agent approval quorum (the deploy gate's `approvals` block in the workflow-v2 shape, ADR-055/ADR-113 D2, with a `not` leg excluding agents) satisfying the repository's count and eligibility predicates authorizes that exact intent. A production-class deploy gate that declares no `approvals` block refuses guarded promotion; the legacy `approvers` role check never authorizes a production-class intent. For a production-class intent every counted approval must present an ADR-123 forge-verified credential kind (`session`, `oauth`, `oauth_as`) whatever predicate legs the gate declares, and the kind is recorded on the approval so that the quorum count and the pre-dispatch re-evaluation honour it; ADR-123's subject-only counting residual does not apply to this class. A gate declaring only `min_permission` or `member_of` does not escape that rule, which for this one class extends ADR-123 Decision 1 beyond the members leg and narrows ADR-055 Decision 1's allowance that static tokens keep working at every surface: a static-token approval never counts toward a production-class intent. An issue-comment approval (ADR-123 Decision 4) presents no credential kind and is not counted toward a production-class quorum, whether or not channel parity exists. As ADR-113 states, this verifies the forge identity, not that a human rather than a process holding that identity's credentials acted; the control against an operator agent approving production is procedural. Approval of a production-class deployment intent is non-delegable: no delegation condition satisfies it, including an acceptance-pass condition such as the illustrative `may_approve_deploy: acceptance_passed` of ADR-049 Recommendation 6. Deployment to a development or qualification-class target keeps ADR-038's gate and ADR-040's delegation knobs unchanged; only production-class approval is made non-delegable. Where a repository's gate declares `author`, the author is the subject the existing change-author resolver returns (`backend/internal/server/quorum.go:190`), and an unresolved author refuses rather than disabling that leg for a production-class intent, which for this one class is stricter than the stage resolver's unresolved-author behaviour (`backend/internal/server/quorum.go:244-254`). A repository whose production-class gate sits on a deploy-only workflow declares `not: [agent]`, as this one does for ratification (ADR-113 D2), because the change-author resolver reads a run's vouched commits and a deploy-only run has none; this repository's `release` deploy gate declares `not: [author, agent]` today (`.fishhawk/workflows.yaml:1299`) and must drop `author` before production enablement, an operator-authored change. This record neither reduces a configured quorum nor requires a second approver where only one is configured. Provider-required environment approvals remain independent controls. Changing a bound field, the predecessor, required evidence, or relevant authority invalidates pending authorization. Time-bound evidence and expiry use authoritative recorded times, not an agent's assertion. Re-evaluation immediately before dispatch closes stale-approval gaps.

A release surface shows qualification, authorization, rollout outcome, and production smoke outcome separately. A successful deployment stage is not synonymous with a production-verified release. Recovery creates a new observation/attempt; it does not overwrite the failed history.

### D5. Dispatch is idempotent and fenced per target

Use a durable intent/attempt identity and an atomic authorization-to-dispatch claim before any external request. The intent is the payload of the deploy stage's pre-execution gate (ADR-038); an attempt is a dispatch of that stage, recorded durably at the authorization-to-dispatch claim and referenced by the `deployment` artifact the outcome later writes. Environment registration composes with `allowed_environments`: both must admit the target. Repeated calls with the same intent reconcile the existing attempt. A timeout after sending a request means dispatch status is unknown; look up by correlation before sending again. If the provider cannot prove whether it accepted the dispatch, hold for reconciliation rather than risk a duplicate side effect.

Every target-changing path participates in a per-environment generation/fencing contract: forward deploy, rollback, retry, manual workflow dispatch, declared emergency recovery, and any removal or disabling of a deployed artifact, which uses the same intent and fence once its schema extension is specified. The external pipeline validates the intent, expected predecessor, and current fence before acting. A stale worker cannot deploy after a later authorized attempt acquires the target. Application of this fence in the real platform is an enablement prerequisite, not something a backend lock alone proves.

Deployment serialization is per environment, not a repository-wide lock on development or review. It does not modify ADR-115's pre-merge parallelism or the merge queue. An external out-of-band deployment breaks the observed generation, invalidates pending qualification/approval, and holds promotion until reconciled. Break-glass recovery stays possible through the customer's authority, but is recorded honestly as external recovery, not retroactively approved.

### D6. Promote the qualified artifacts and observe actual identity

The external pipeline consumes the approved artifact digests and deployment intent. It may adapt explicitly declared environment configuration, but cannot rebuild the application or resolve an unbound mutable tag for production. Before approval, qualification records the differences between tested and intended production configuration and assesses their effect on each blocking criterion. A difference that invalidates a criterion's applicability requires new evidence; an unexplained difference holds promotion. Signature/provenance verification binds the artifacts to the permitted builder and source under the adopted policy. The pipeline remains the owner of deployment mechanics and production credentials.

The resulting observation names the intent/attempt, target identity/generation, actual artifact digests, applied configuration and migration revisions, execution identity, provider run/attempt, timestamps, outcome, and recovery reference. Correlate by exact IDs, not newest workflow run or branch name.

Authenticate reports through the adopted provider/workload identity and verify its repository, workflow, immutable executable revision, attempt, and target authorization. The evidence channel authenticates a registered production smoke checker (ADR-133 D5, #4899) as a principal distinct from the target's rollout delegate. Reject cross-target/cross-repository substitutions and replay; an identical duplicate is idempotent, a conflicting duplicate holds the result. The target's observed identity comes from the registered platform verifier, not a model's narrative or the application's untrusted self-report alone. Inability to establish identity yields unknown and blocks a verified-release claim.

Fishhawk need not fetch customer source or runtime. A customer-side verifier observes the platform and ships bounded authenticated evidence. This authenticates who observed what under which adopted checker; it does not prove that a compromised authorized pipeline told the truth. Protecting that pipeline and checker is part of the threat model.

### D7. Recovery names the exact deployment and preserves data semantics

A recovery request targets a deployment intent/attempt and environment, not implicitly the run's first deploy stage. The current run-scoped rollback selection (ADR-053 Decision §1's `rollback_handle` actioning, E35.3) is amended: a recovery request selects by deployment intent/attempt and environment, and the run-scoped endpoint is retained only as a compatibility alias that refuses when the selection is ambiguous. This ADR therefore also amends ADR-053. The request records the intended recovery artifact, expected current generation, recovery procedure, data-compatibility preconditions, and authorizing decision.

The pipeline may perform a previously approved automatic rollout recovery such as Helm atomic rollback within that intent. Otherwise a rollback or roll-forward on a production-class target is itself authorized as a production-class deployment intent under D4: a forge-verified, non-agent, non-delegable approval, which the run-scoped `mcp:run` exemption from `write:deploy` (`backend/internal/server/deploy_rollback.go:102-108`) does not satisfy; the run-scoped compatibility alias is held to the same authorization as the request it resolves to. A failed migration or partially applied change never triggers an unbounded blind redeploy. Irreversible data changes require a forward-recovery plan and separately authorized destructive actions.

Rollback initiation, pipeline completion, observed resulting identity, and recovery verification are separate evidence. Failed or unknown recovery cannot produce a successful `rolled_back` claim. A conflicting external recovery requires reconciliation, not another automatic attempt. Restore drills and retained recovery artifacts are required where the release promises recoverability.

### D8. Release publication does not rebuild or rewrite history

Amend ADR-051's publication contract to link evidence-derived notes and published versions to the immutable candidate. Human-owned version/tag decisions remain. The tag/publication pipeline must promote or package the already qualified bytes where feasible without changing their identity. If packaging/signing changes the deployable bytes, that result is a new candidate requiring qualification before production.

This repository's tag-driven path builds and publishes in one run (`.github/workflows/backend-release.yml:7-18`, `:24-27`), so it cannot qualify a candidate before publication. Qualifying a candidate on that path needs a pipeline that builds an unpublished candidate and publishes it at a later explicit step; that changes the release workflows ADR-073 Decision §4 holds fixed. That change is ADR-137 (#4926), a separate record that amends ADR-073 §4; until it is implemented this repository's tag-driven Release qualifies no candidate and publishes none of the qualified bytes. A candidate assembled from a `backend-build.yml` main-head image (`ghcr.io/kuhlman-labs/fishhawkd:sha-<full>`, cosign-signed by digest; `.github/workflows/backend-build.yml:59`, `:95`) is not excluded by §4, since qualifying it changes neither workflow; whether the published Release must carry that same digest is ADR-137's question. ADR-131 D4 (#4897) treats no digest in this repository as qualified until ADR-137 is implemented; the preceding sentence states only what §4 itself excludes. Publication follows qualification, because a tag-built Release is immutable once published (`.github/workflows/backend-release.yml:24-27`). Until ADR-137 is implemented, this repository's tag path is labelled legacy/unqualified under D9 and qualifies no candidate. Never claim build-on-tag and rebuild-on-promotion preserve identical artifacts without proof.

Published GitHub Releases remain immutable under ADR-051's existing amendment. Finalize notes/assets before publication; later corrections use a separately attributed correction mechanism supported by the provider. Publishing a release does not dispatch production implicitly. A production deployment does not imply that release notes were published.

### D9. Scope, compatibility, and incremental enablement

The product contract is repository- and provider-neutral (ADR-110). A first provider implementation can be explicitly scoped to GitHub Actions plus one external verifier. Unsupported delegate capabilities refuse guarded promotion by name; the system cannot silently fall back to ref-only dispatch.

Enable this contract explicitly per workflow/target. Existing deploy workflows keep their documented behavior and are labelled legacy/unqualified rather than retroactively certified. Existing acceptance shapes remain valid. New artifact/schema/API fields follow the additive-versus-major rules; no sixth stage type or new acceptance executor is introduced. For this repository, adopting production promotion requires migrating every authorized production entry point, not leaving a legacy bypass beside the new gate. Until a qualification-class target is registered, guarded production promotion is unavailable. The verification-policy binding in D4 is omitted until a production verification policy is adopted.

### Amendment scope

Each amended record changes only as named here; its other decisions stand.

- **ADR-038:** exact-intent dispatch and recovery. The deploy stage's pre-execution gate carries the D4 intent, dispatch follows D5, and recovery follows D7.
- **ADR-040:** delegation. No `operator_agent` delegation condition satisfies approval of a production-class deployment intent (D4).
- **ADR-049:** candidate-bound release qualification (D3), while preserving preview acceptance. An acceptance run may take its criteria from the ratified brief rather than an approved plan (Recommendation 4; Decision refinement 3), where the D3 mapping names the brief's own criteria. No delegation condition satisfies production-class approval, including an acceptance-pass condition such as the illustrative `may_approve_deploy: acceptance_passed` of ADR-049 Recommendation 6 (D4).
- **ADR-051:** immutable candidate publication (D8).
- **ADR-053:** rollback selection only (D7). Its Decision §3 staging-only acceptance boundary is unchanged (D3).
- **ADR-055:** static-token approvals. A static-token approval never counts toward a production-class intent, narrowing Decision 1's allowance that static tokens keep working at every surface; that allowance stands for every other gate (D4).
- **ADR-079:** readiness binds to the exact brief, candidate, and qualification revisions (D4).
- **ADR-123:** the credential-kind floor extends to every counted approval on a production-class gate, whatever legs it declares; the kind is recorded on the approval, the subject-only counting residual does not apply to this class, and an issue-comment approval (Decision 4) is not counted toward a production-class quorum (D4).

## Decision

**Accepted 2026-11-04** by the Captain (1 of 1; manual ratification path, the ADR-113 D2 verb is not yet built; approver and hashes in the ratification comment). **Option B**, i.e. D1–D9 as stated in the Recommendation, decided now. It amends ADR-038, ADR-040, ADR-049, ADR-051, ADR-053, ADR-055, ADR-079 and ADR-123 only as its Amendment scope states. Guarded production promotion is unavailable until ADR-121's bindings and this contract are implemented, and this repository's tag-driven Release qualifies no candidate until ADR-137 (#4926) is implemented. This record enables no production deployment, starts no work and grants no standing authority.

## Consequences

The release has one navigable evidence chain without making Fishhawk a build/deploy platform. The costs are real product work: immutable manifest and intent storage, environment registration, approval binding, evidence authenticity/freshness, dispatch fencing/reconciliation, exact recovery selection, and clear operator state. Human approval is meaningful only if the delegated pipeline enforces the binding too.

Implementation must specify the wire protocols, schemas, authentication/rotation, retention, supported provider capabilities, secret-reference treatment, and transition rules before building each slice, and satisfy the repository's Auth change checklist for every new ingress or scope, including the auth tightening of the existing `POST /v0/runs/{run_id}/deployment` route (`docs/api/v0.md:5173`, `docs/api/v0.md:5203-5208`) that D6's authenticated reports require. Record new audit categories and cross-module contracts in their canonical registries. Required data that cannot be safely exposed is represented by an authorized opaque reference; source ACLs govern every linked view. Missing private evidence cannot be counted as passing in an unauthorized partial view.

No new alpha blocker or production authority is created by ratification. Installations and live production walks require separately approved configuration after validation. A three-environment profile for this repository is decided separately by ADR-131 (#4897, an ADR-073 amendment); this record does not depend on it.

- **Identity residual.** A process holding an approver's forge credentials can approve a production-class intent as that approver (ADR-113; ADR-121 D6). The control is procedural.
- **Author separation.** On a deploy-only production-class gate declaring `not: [agent]`, the candidate's author is not excluded from approving it, because no author resolves on that run; the controls are the count, any `members` list the gate declares, the other predicate legs, and the forge-verified credential floor. This repository's `release` gate must drop `author` (`not: [agent]`), an operator-authored change, because D4 refuses a production-class intent on an unresolved author; the accepted consequence is that a candidate's author can approve its production deploy.
- **Deferred.** The ADR-073 §4 amendment D8 relies on is ADR-137 (#4926), not decided by this record; until it is implemented this repository's tag-driven Release qualifies no candidate and publishes none of the qualified bytes.

### Validation and delivery gates

1. A synthetic non-Fishhawk repository builds two components and qualifies a candidate against a declared environment. Different declarations change behavior through production resolvers.
2. The same source is rebuilt to different bytes; a mutable tag moves; the pipeline tries another digest: all invalidate or refuse the approved deployment.
3. Criteria, target configuration, checker revision, credential reference, registration, or predecessor changes between approval and dispatch: authorization is held, not silently transferred.
4. A callback is duplicated, conflicts, is replayed from another attempt, or names another repository/target: identity checks reject or hold appropriately.
5. A dispatch acknowledgement is lost or the backend restarts after sending: exactly the known external attempt is reconciled; no blind duplicate dispatch.
6. Two deployments, a rollback, and a manual pipeline compete for the same target: only the valid fence can act; unrelated development remains parallel.
7. Pipeline success with a wrong/unverifiable artifact cannot produce a qualified deployment. Missing or zero required checks cannot produce qualification success.
8. A failed migration, unavailable rollback handle, failed rollback, or forward-incompatible database produces an explicit recovery state, never a fabricated successful rollback.
9. A tag-driven release publishes immutable assets and evidence without rebuilding the promoted candidate or attempting an unsupported published-Release rewrite.
10. A complete operator walk demonstrates intent → qualification → approval → dispatch → observed outcome → recovery, including restart and stale-evidence cases, before production enablement.
11. Environment registration, production approval, and non-preauthorized recovery are tested against their applicable identity, delegation, eligibility, quorum, expiry and revocation rules; invalid authorization is refused and audited before an external side effect, and registration without tier-I adoption is refused. A production-class deploy gate that declares no `approvals` block refuses guarded promotion, and the legacy `approvers` role check does not authorize the intent. Observation reports are tested separately against D6's producer authentication, execution and target binding, replay and conflict rules. A production smoke result submitted under the rollout delegate's identity is refused. A valid authenticated report of an already-dispatched attempt remains recordable after its deployment approval expires, without renewing authority or unblocking subsequent promotion. An approver who no longer satisfies the predicate at dispatch is dropped before the intent dispatches, and an authorization-record persistence failure prevents dispatch. Every legacy entry point for an enabled target is exercised, including the run-scoped rollback route and its same-run `mcp:run` exemption from `write:deploy` (`backend/internal/server/deploy_rollback.go:102-108`, `backend/internal/server/deploy_rollback.go:129-147`, `backend/internal/server/deploy_rollback.go:446-452`); both are entry points to migrate before enablement.
12. An unknown target or class refuses guarded deployment. Resolving a registered production target through prod, production or a hostname alias preserves its production class: an attempt without valid production authorization refuses, while an otherwise valid authorized intent remains deployable. A candidate edit to its own environment registration is held for adoption.
13. With the contract enabled, a tag push, a default-branch ref dispatch and a manual workflow dispatch lacking a valid exact-intent authorization and current fence each fail to change the production target; an authorized dispatch through a supported channel deploys only the approved artifacts to the approved target.
14. A re-qualification with no registered reuse rule re-executes every check.
15. Registration or authority is revoked after dispatch: the actual outcome and the uncertainty are preserved, subsequent promotion is held, and recovery is routed to the operator, with no fabricated cancellation or rollback.
16. An acceptance stage consuming an enabled production-class target spelled as an alias is rejected.
17. A production-class gate declaring `author` refuses on an unresolved author and excludes the subject the change-author resolver returns.
18. A static token with a forge-shaped subject is refused on a production-class gate that declares no `members`.
19. A pre-deployment blocking criterion attributed to a development-class layer with no qualification-class evidence mapping holds guarded promotion; its development-layer evidence stays informational and does not change the criterion's ratified blocking status.
20. On a production-class gate, an approval row carries its recorded credential kind; a pre-existing or open-gate static-credential approval and an issue-comment approval are each excluded from the quorum count and from the pre-dispatch re-evaluation.
